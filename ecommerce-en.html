Business Intelligence, Amazon PPC & Growth Economics

Growth architecture for an auto parts ecommerce portfolio

In this case I worked on a mature ecommerce portfolio where the problem was not simply “selling more”, but understanding what part of the growth came from real demand, what part depended on paid investment and what part could be sustained by structural improvements in conversion, organic ranking and commercial efficiency.

The focus was to build a business reading rather than an isolated reading of campaigns. I crossed weekly sales, organic traffic, paid traffic, conversion, performance by product and advertising profitability to detect where the system was gaining traction and where it was only buying volume. From that reading, PPC's work ceased to be bid management and began to function as a capital allocation mechanism: investing where margin, search intent, and conversion probability justified scaling.

The result was a simultaneous improvement in scale and traffic quality. In the semi-annual comparison, retail sales grew 26.3%, total traffic grew 19.4% and Paid Search traffic increased 119.4%, without the analysis being reduced to spending more: the central point was to use BI to distinguish defensible terms, products with positive elasticity and areas where the advertising budget could boost ranking, conversion and incremental revenue.

The economic hypothesis behind the work was simple: in competitive marketplaces, profitable growth appears when ads, indexable content, pricing, fitment and availability operate as the same system. That is why the analysis was not limited to the immediate ROAS, but looked at the effect of each decision on organic traffic, conversion rate, keyword coverage and the portfolio's ability to sustain sales when paid pressure drops.

  • +26,3% Retail sales in semiannual comparison
  • +119,4% Paid Search Traffic
  • +19,4% Total Traffic
  • +5,5% Average improvement in conversion rate
