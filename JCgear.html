Amazon Account Management Case Study

How I Helped Improve Amazon Growth, Advertising Efficiency, and Marketplace Operations for Daizzy Gear

This case study shows how I contributed to the growth and operational improvement of an Amazon brand by combining Amazon PPC management, listing optimization, inventory-aware advertising, promotional planning, storefront updates, and data-driven marketplace strategy.

My work focused on turning fragmented account activity into a more structured Amazon growth system. Campaigns were optimized around product availability and return potential, promotions were built to move aged inventory, content was updated to support conversion, and advertising decisions were tied directly to business priorities.

The Challenge

Daizzy Gear needed more than isolated Amazon Ads adjustments. The account required a full-marketplace approach that connected PPC, catalog structure, inventory status, discounts, A+ Content, Storefront merchandising, and product-level prioritization.

Several issues had to be managed at the same time: low or uneven inventory across key SKUs, aged inventory that needed sell-through support, Prime Day preparation, product variations that required different advertising treatment, and the need to concentrate budget on the items most likely to convert.

Instead of treating Amazon PPC as a standalone channel, I approached it as part of a broader Amazon account management system. The goal was to improve visibility, protect profitability, support inventory planning, and help the brand make better decisions from its own performance data.

What I Worked On

My contribution covered both Amazon advertising and general marketplace execution. I helped manage Sponsored Products campaigns, keyword targeting, bid optimization, search term analysis, promotional setup, Storefront content, A+ Content, Virtual Bundles, inventory movement, parent-child variation structure, and weekly performance reporting.

The work was especially focused on identifying which products deserved additional investment and which areas needed to be reduced, paused, or restructured. This meant looking beyond surface metrics and analyzing how spend, ROAS, ACOS, TACOS, CPC, conversion behavior, stock status, and catalog positioning interacted with each other.

Amazon PPC Strategy and Advertising Optimization

A major part of the work was improving the structure and efficiency of Amazon Ads campaigns. I reviewed campaign performance by product, adjusted bids, refined targeting, and shifted spend toward campaigns with stronger conversion potential.

Before Prime Day, the account was prepared by strengthening campaign coverage across priority products. I used recent performance trends to decide where to increase visibility, where to protect budget, and where spend should be limited. This included multiple rounds of bid optimization, keyword refinement, and campaign budget adjustments.

The strategy was not simply to spend more. The objective was to spend more intelligently. Campaigns and search terms that showed weaker returns were reduced or paused, while products with better ROAS potential and available inventory received more support.

  • Search term performance analysis
  • Budget reallocation from weak campaigns to stronger opportunities
  • ACOS, TACOS, ROAS, CPC, impressions, clicks, and sales monitoring
  • Inventory-aware PPC scaling
  • Prime Day advertising preparation and post-event optimization

Results and Business Impact

The account showed clear positive movement after applying a more controlled, data-driven Amazon strategy. During one key reporting period, weekly ordered revenue reached approximately $2,415, representing a 176.4% increase versus the prior week.

During the Prime Day period, the brand generated approximately $2,504 in sales. Even though a payment issue temporarily paused advertising and limited the ability to fully capture Prime Day demand, sales momentum recovered once ads resumed. This showed that the account structure and campaign preparation were strong enough to regain traction when the issue was resolved.

The advertising work also revealed which products were actually resonating with customers. Among the discounted ASINs, the retractable leashes were the clear sales driver, which helped guide future budget allocation, promotional planning, and product prioritization.

Inventory-Aware Amazon Advertising

One of the most important improvements was connecting advertising decisions with inventory reality. Campaigns were not managed in isolation from stock levels. When Pink and Blue Retractable Leash inventory was limited, ads were paused or controlled to avoid driving demand into poor fulfillment conditions. Once inventory was replenished and distributed through FBA, the advertising strategy shifted back toward growth.

This helped reduce wasted spend and protected the customer experience. It also made the PPC strategy more commercially useful, because every advertising decision was tied to whether the product could actually be sold, shipped quickly, and scaled profitably.

I also supported FBA replenishment planning, including the movement of Pink and Blue Retractable Leashes from AWD to FBA warehouses. This was important because Amazon advertising performance is closely connected to availability, delivery speed, Buy Box strength, and conversion rate.

Promotions, Coupons, and Aged Inventory Sell-Through

Another key area of impact was promotional strategy. I helped implement a combination of 10% coupons and 15% price discounts to accelerate the sell-through of aged inventory without relying on a single deep discount across every order.

This approach made the offer more visible on Amazon by combining the coupon badge with the price discount display. It allowed the brand to create a stronger perceived deal while still protecting margin compared with a blanket 25% discount.

The Green Retractable Leash was included in the promotion because it was planned for discontinuation. That decision connected advertising, pricing, and inventory liquidation into one coordinated marketplace action.

Content, Storefront, and Conversion Support

I also contributed to the account beyond PPC by supporting content improvements designed to improve conversion and merchandising. This included Storefront updates, promotional pages, A+ Content banners, comparison charts, product video uploads, and Prime Day-specific creative placements.

For Prime Day, a dedicated Storefront page was created for deals, and Prime Day banners were added to A+ Content. For ongoing promotions, a Limited Time page and Exclusive Discounts banner helped direct shoppers toward active offers.

These updates mattered because Amazon Ads can only perform well when the listing experience supports the traffic. By improving content and merchandising, the account had a stronger foundation for conversion, not just more impressions.

Catalog and Account Management Improvements

The work also included practical Amazon account management actions that directly affected performance. These included creating Virtual Bundles, merging retractable leashes back under the correct parent ASIN, adjusting product titles, creating removal orders for discontinued or aged inventory, and preparing market research for new product categories.

For example, the Retractable Leashes were merged back under a parent ASIN to help the variations regain momentum and support cross-variation sales. New Virtual Bundles were also created by pairing Pink and Blue retractable leashes with harness sets, giving the brand more ways to increase average order value and present related products together.

These changes show my broader Amazon marketplace experience: I do not only manage ads, I understand how catalog structure, content, fulfillment, promotions, and product strategy affect performance.

How I Think About Amazon Growth

My approach to Amazon growth is based on connecting the numbers with the operational reality of the account. A high ROAS campaign is not useful if the product is running out of stock. A promotion is not enough if the Storefront does not support it. A listing update has limited value if traffic quality is poor. A campaign budget increase can hurt the account if it is not tied to search term data and conversion behavior.

That is why I focus on the full system: Amazon PPC, SEO, catalog structure, inventory, promotions, Storefront, reporting, and business priorities. This allows me to make decisions that are practical for the brand, not just technically correct inside Ads Manager.

Why This Matters for Amazon Brands

Many Amazon accounts lose money because their PPC strategy is separated from the rest of the business. Ads are scaled while inventory is low, poor-converting products keep receiving spend, promotions are not supported by content, and performance reports describe what happened without clearly explaining what should change.

In this account, I helped create a more connected operating model. Advertising decisions were linked to product-level performance, inventory availability, promotional timing, and content readiness. That made the account easier to manage and gave the brand a clearer path toward profitable growth.

For recruiters, agencies, or Amazon brands looking for support, this case demonstrates my ability to manage both the analytical and operational sides of Amazon marketplace growth. I can optimize campaigns, but I can also understand the business context behind those campaigns.

Summary

My work with Daizzy Gear helped improve Amazon account execution through better PPC management, sharper budget allocation, stronger promotional planning, improved Storefront and A+ Content support, inventory-aware advertising, and more structured weekly decision-making.

The positive impact came from using Amazon Ads data as a business tool, not just a reporting dashboard. By connecting campaign performance with catalog structure, inventory, promotions, and customer-facing content, I helped the brand make smarter decisions and build a stronger foundation for marketplace growth.
