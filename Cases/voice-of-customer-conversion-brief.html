Voice of Customer · Ecommerce Research · CRO

From review noise to a conversion brief.

This anonymized project shows how I turn customer language into creative direction for a medical-consumable listing. The goal was not to decorate a gallery. It was to identify what shoppers need to believe, what they fear, and which claims require visible proof.

Research framework

Four steps from language to layout.

01

Cluster the language

Group repeated phrases into outcomes, objections, use contexts and expectations instead of treating every review as an isolated anecdote.

02

Separate desire from proof

“Pain-free,” “gentle” and “effective” are desired outcomes. A content brief still needs a credible demonstration or substantiation plan.

03

Rank decision friction

Prioritize the doubts most likely to delay purchase: effectiveness, residue, odor, skin comfort, wipe size and moisture consistency.

04

Map each claim to a frame

Assign every gallery image or video one job: establish the product, demonstrate removal, clarify use, prove scale or resolve an objection.

The creative brief

A gallery sequence built around confidence.

  1. 01 · Product clarity

    Lead with a clean pack presentation and immediately identify the product as individually packaged adhesive-remover wipes.

  2. 02 · Claim to proof

    Demonstrate removal with a realistic sequence. Avoid unsupported “instant” or absolute performance language.

  3. 03 · Skin experience

    Explain the intended gentle-use proposition while keeping regulatory and substantiation constraints visible to the team.

  4. 04 · Objection handling

    Use one frame to address residue, odor and post-use comfort—the frictions customers mention after the main task.

  5. 05 · Relevant scenarios

    Prioritize medical tape, dressings and device-related adhesive. Do not dilute the story with every edge-case use discovered in reviews.

  6. 06 · Expectation setting

    Show wipe dimensions, pack count and usage steps clearly to reduce size and moisture surprises after delivery.

Research integrity

Review mining finds questions. It does not manufacture proof.

Customer language is excellent for identifying uncertainty and prioritizing communication. It cannot validate medical, safety or performance claims by itself. The responsible workflow is to use review themes to write the test plan, request substantiation and shape the content hierarchy—then publish only what the product can prove.
