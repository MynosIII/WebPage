Search Query Performance & Keyword Harvesting

A repeatable system for moving search terms from discovery into controlled exact, phrase, broad, and negative structures—without mistaking one conversion for proof.

Executive summary

A search term is evidence, not automatically a keyword

Amazon's reports describe different layers of the same demand system. Search Query Performance shows how shoppers move from query impressions toward purchases. Advertising search-term reports reveal which auction matches consumed spend. Targeting reports show how promoted keywords perform after they are given their own bids and match types.

The harvesting decision happens where those layers meet. A term should graduate into a controlled target when it demonstrates relevance, economic viability, and enough repeatable evidence. It should become negative when the account has paid enough to establish mismatch or unacceptable economics—not simply because it failed once.

Evidence boundary: the figures below are anonymized snapshots from separate reports and products. They demonstrate the diagnostic method; they are not presented as one before-and-after experiment.
Signal architecture

Three reports, three different questions

Search Query Performance

Is the brand earning impressions, clicks, cart adds, and purchases for a query across the marketplace journey?

Search-term report

What did the shopper type, which target matched it, and did the resulting traffic create profitable orders?

Targeting report

Once promoted, does the keyword sustain volume and efficiency under its own match type and bid?

Query-level marketplace data gives context; ad reports give controllability. Neither is sufficient alone. High search volume without brand relevance can waste budget, while an efficient low-volume term may deserve protection even if it never appears near the top of a category list.

Marketplace funnel evidence

The brand gained share as shoppers moved deeper into the funnel

Across an anonymized 1,000-query monthly Search Query Performance export, the brand represented 2.54% of 403.2 million marketplace impressions, 3.19% of 6.88 million clicks, 3.24% of 1.82 million cart adds, and 3.72% of 737,810 purchases. The increasing share does not prove advertising caused the purchases, but it does show that the brand's relative performance strengthened after the impression stage.

Observed sample

The long tail consumed most of the tested spend without orders

The 221-row search-term snapshot generated 2,868 impressions, 364 clicks, 23 orders, and $471.33 in attributed sales from $241.93 in spend. That implies a 12.7% click rate, 6.3% click-to-order conversion rate, and 51.3% ACoS for this ad-group export. Only 18 distinct terms converted. Non-converting terms accounted for $159.05—65.7% of the spend in this specific snapshot.

This does not mean every non-converting term should be blocked. Some terms have only one click; others may be relevant discovery traffic. The useful insight is concentration: the account needs explicit thresholds so repeated weak signals do not remain invisible inside aggregate ACoS.

Harvesting workflow

From discovery to controlled allocation

01

Collect

Join query, match, clicks, spend, orders, sales, and conversion signals.

02

Normalize

Clean plurals, variants, spelling, product identifiers, and duplicates.

03

Classify

Separate product intent, problem intent, competitor intent, and mismatch.

04

Promote

Move proven terms into a target with its own bid and budget logic.

05

Negate

Prevent discovery campaigns from competing with or relearning resolved traffic.

Harvest score = relevance confidence × conversion evidence × margin fit × repeatability

The score is a decision model, not a universal Amazon formula. The weighting should change with price, margin, conversion rate, click cost, launch stage, and the account's tolerance for exploration.

Economics

Set thresholds from break-even conversion, not habit

Break-even ACoS = contribution margin ÷ revenueBreak-even CVR = CPC ÷ contribution per order

If a product can contribute $8 per order and the expected CPC is $0.80, the traffic needs roughly a 10% conversion rate to break even before broader strategic considerations. That gives the analyst a defensible way to judge whether ten clicks without an order are acceptable, premature, or already expensive.

A separate targeting snapshot illustrates why controlled targets matter: 136 exact targets produced 992 clicks, 147 orders, $2,965.52 in sales, and 31.4% ACoS. Because this is a different product/report snapshot, it is not a causal comparison with the search-term sample. It does show the richer measurement available once each target has its own history.

Operating cadence

Harvesting is a closed loop, not a cleanup task

Weekly

Review meaningful spend, new converters, semantic mismatch, duplicate routing, and sudden CPC changes.

Monthly

Reconcile ad data with Search Query Performance and Search Catalog Performance to find organic and conversion gaps.

Quarterly

Rebuild intent clusters, retire stale targets, and reassess thresholds against price, margin, inventory, and category change.

Source material curated for this framework: Amazon Sponsored Products search-term and targeting exports, Search Query Performance Brand View, Search Catalog Performance, and Top Search Terms reports.

Conclusion

The goal is not more keywords. It is better query ownership.

A mature harvesting system keeps discovery active while steadily improving control. Broad and automatic targeting learn; exact and phrase structures allocate; negatives prevent waste and overlap; query-performance reports reveal whether advertising gains are translating into stronger marketplace presence.

The best outcome is not the largest keyword list. It is a portfolio in which every target has a role, every promotion has evidence, and every negative reflects a documented decision.
