Amazon Listing Audit Checklist

What to verify before increasing ad spend: a complete diagnostic sequence for separating discoverability, conversion, catalog, operational, and profitability problems.

Start here

Do not optimize the visible copy before proving the listing can sell.

A listing audit is a dependency check. Advertising cannot compensate for a suppressed ASIN, a lost Buy Box, unavailable inventory, weak delivery promise, broken variation, or an offer that cannot convert profitably. The first task is to identify which layer is constraining performance.

1. Retail readinessLive, purchasable, in stock, compliant.
2. RelevanceIndexed for the right demand.
3. ConversionClear, credible, competitive.
4. EconomicsMargin supports acquisition.
01 / Retail readiness

Can the shopper actually complete the purchase?

Listing status

Confirm the ASIN is active, searchable, category-correct, and free from suppression or detail-page removal.

Buy Box and offer

Check featured-offer eligibility, price consistency, seller status, and whether another offer owns the conversion path.

Inventory and fulfillment

Review FBA/FBM availability, inbound stock, stranded units, delivery promise, and imminent stockout risk.

Variation integrity

Verify parent-child theme, child labels, swatches, review sharing, and that each variation matches the customer's selection logic.

02 / Search relevance

Does Amazon understand when this product belongs?

Keyword map

Prioritize the intersection of demand and relevance. Separate category heads, product attributes, use cases, audiences, and long-tail modifiers.

Title logic

Lead with product identity and decision-critical attributes. Preserve human syntax while making the primary search intent unmistakable.

Bullet coverage

Map one buyer question to each bullet: configuration, compatibility, use, construction, care, guarantee, or proof.

Backend and indexation

Check missing synonyms, duplicate terms, restricted claims, category attributes, and manual indexation for priority queries.

From the operating handbook: high volume alone is not enough; the target is the intersection of volume and relevance, with long-tail terms useful in highly competitive markets.
03 / Conversion

Can a shopper resolve uncertainty without leaving the page?

SurfaceAudit questionTypical failure
Main imageIs the product immediately legible at thumbnail size?Low visual occupancy, unclear pack quantity, distracting additions.
Supplemental imagesDo they answer dimensions, fit, setup, materials, use, and objections?Decorative repetition instead of decision support.
A+ contentDoes it extend the argument with comparison, proof, brand, and product-family logic?Duplicated gallery copy or an attractive but non-informative layout.
Reviews and returnsWhat expectation gap repeats in critical reviews and return reasons?The listing promises broadly but fails to prevent a predictable mismatch.
Competitive offerAre price, coupon, rating, delivery, and feature claims competitive?Content optimized in isolation from the current search-results page.
04 / Performance diagnosis

Use the funnel to locate the loss.

Impressions down

Investigate indexation, organic rank, search share, PPC coverage, category placement, stock, and suppression.

Clicks down

Compare main image, title, price, coupon, rating, delivery promise, badge, and competing offers on the SERP.

Conversion down

Inspect detail-page communication, reviews, returns, price, offer stability, variation clarity, and competitor changes.

Profit down

Reconcile price, fees, landed cost, ad spend, returns, reimbursements, promotions, and inventory carrying costs.

The archived product review reinforces this sequence: a contribution-margin decline was checked against stock, FBA fees, units, revenue, reimbursements, organic/paid mix, return rate, keyword rank, conversion, and PPC before actions were proposed. That breadth prevents one metric from becoming a premature diagnosis.

Anonymized audit evidence

A margin alert required reconciliation, not a copy rewrite.

In one archived monthly product audit, units declined from 4,105 to 2,049 (-50.1%), revenue moved from $73,631 to $36,775 (-50.1%), and contribution margin fell from $14,530 to $5,282 (-63.6%). The sharper margin decline justified investigation, but operational checks found the offer in stock and the FBA-fee movement immaterial.

SignalObserved evidenceInterpretation
DemandUnits and revenue both declined about 50%The first-order problem was traffic or demand volume, not only checkout conversion.
ProfitabilityContribution margin declined faster than revenuePricing, ad cost, fees, and mix required separate reconciliation.
ReturnsReturn rate improved from 1.05% to 0.63%Returns did not explain the commercial decline in this period.
VisibilityFour priority terms held top organic positions; several also held top sponsored positionsA broad claim of lost keyword rank was not supported by the reviewed terms.
AdvertisingACoS was near the operating threshold, with inefficient exact targets identifiedSelective bid and target changes were more defensible than pausing all PPC.
Evidence boundary: figures are rounded and product identity is omitted. This is one monthly audit snapshot, not proof that any single listing component caused the decline.
05 / Scoring and action

Turn findings into a prioritized change log.

ScoreMeaningAction
0Broken or blockingFix before spending: suppression, no offer, stockout, wrong variation, compliance risk.
1Material weaknessPrioritize in the next controlled change cycle.
2Acceptable, improvableTest only with a defined hypothesis and success metric.
3StrongPreserve; avoid changing a working component without evidence.

Record the baseline, exact change, date, affected ASINs, expected mechanism, primary metric, guardrail metrics, and review window. A change without a measurement plan creates activity—not learning.
